## Assessment

No earlier statement of Tuza's inequality for **all** line graphs of finite
simple graphs, for **all** total graphs of finite simple graphs, or of the
claimed cubic triangle-free total-graph cover/packing equality was located.
This is a search result, not a proof of novelty. The manuscript's cautious
"not aware" wording is supportable; a definitive first-proof claim is not.

Two construction attributions need strengthening before circulation:

1. The alternating Euler-tour coloring belongs to the established line-graph
   maximum-cut method of Guruswami (1999). Cite it where the method is introduced.
2. The modular clique packing used for finite bases is the construction
   attributed to Sebő in Munaro's thesis, Theorem 3.3.2. Attribute it directly,
   rather than merely mentioning a comparison with that theorem.

These findings concern credit for ingredients. The passages accessed do not
state the manuscript's two full headline Tuza inequalities. The extent to
which the new argument is an immediate consequence of the original maximum-cut
paper still merits checking against that paper's full text: publisher access
failed in this pass. Mathematical correctness, novelty, significance, formal
verification, and editorial acceptance remain separate questions.

## Opened sources and precise scope

### 1. Closest line-graph theorem and clique construction

Andrea Munaro, *Sur quelques invariants classiques et nouveaux des
hypergraphes*, doctoral thesis, Université Grenoble Alpes, 2016; defended
1 December 2016. [Author's complete PDF](https://andreamunaro.github.io/munaro-thesis.pdf).

- Theorem 3.3.2, p. 63 (PDF 71), proves the clique inequality with the modular
  packing used in this manuscript. Its credit is Sebő [172]; bibliography
  p. 139 (PDF 147) identifies [172] as **A. Sebő, Personal communication, 2014**.
  Cite the public thesis, not a supposed direct communication with this author.
- Corollary 3.3.3, p. 64 (PDF 72), states the edge version of Tuza for line
  graphs whose roots are triangle-free. Thus the current introduction's
  specific location and hypothesis are correct.
- The thesis's claw-free statements do not establish Tuza for every claw-free
  original graph. Some concern the **triangle graph**, and others concern
  Jones's cycle problem.

**873**, 38–46 (2021),
[doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2021.04.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2021.04.023).

### 3. Nearby K4-free work, not an all-claw-free theorem

Andrea Munaro, “Triangle Packings and Transversals of Some K4-Free Graphs,”
*Graphs and Combinatorics* **34**(4), 647–668 (2018),
[doi:10.1007/s00373-018-1903-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-018-1903-y).
[Opened accepted manuscript with repository bibliographic cover](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/186487135/manuscript.pdf).

Theorem 3 is stated on p. 4 (PDF 5) and proved on pp. 13–14 (PDF 14–15):
the relevant assumptions are on a K4-free original graph and its
(K5, claw)-free **triangle graph**. The statement gives a 3/2 bound under
those assumptions, not a theorem for every claw-free original graph.
Current bibliography metadata and the introduction's broad description match.

### 4. Another line-graph false positive uses vertex parameters

Andrea Munaro, “On Line Graphs of Subcubic Triangle-Free Graphs,”
*Discrete Mathematics* **340**(6), 1210–1226 (2017),
[doi:10.1016/j.disc.2017.01.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2017.01.006).
[Opened accepted manuscript](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/186487120/dmline.pdf).
The triangle packing/transversal parameters defined on p. 2 (PDF 3) use
**vertices** and vertex-disjoint triangles. Those statements must not be
transferred to the edge parameters in the present paper. This source need
not be added to the bibliography merely because its title contains line graphs.

### 5. Perfect triangle graphs do not subsume the headline classes

Aparna Lakshmanan S., Csilla Bujtás, Zsolt Tuza, “Induced cycles in triangle
graphs,” *Discrete Applied Mathematics* **209**, 264–275 (2016),
[doi:10.1016/j.dam.2015.12.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2015.12.012).
[Opened arXiv:1410.8807v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.8807).
Theorem 18, p. 24, proves Tuza when the **triangle graph** is perfect.
The following note, pp. 24–25, discusses the larger odd-wheel-free class
arising from Puleo's work. These are not statements about total graphs;
the notation T(G) there means the triangle graph.

Independent scope check: K5 is a line graph, L(K1,5). Its triangle graph
contains an induced 5-cycle: take the complements of the five consecutive
edges of a 5-cycle on its vertex set. T(K1,4), with T now denoting the
**total** graph, contains a K5, so its triangle graph likewise contains that
induced 5-cycle. Thus perfect triangle graphs do not contain either full
headline class. Larger complete-graph examples in both classes also contain
odd wheels. This is a direct mathematical non-containment check, not a
literature claim.

## Cubic equality and significance

No source for the full equality tau(T(G)) = nu(T(G)) = 5|V(G)|/2 for every
cubic triangle-free simple root was located. Search results about cycle
multiplicity concern edge-disjoint **cycles of arbitrary lengths**, which is
a different parameter and does not supply the required triangle transversal.

Independent scope observation: the canonical bridge triangles, together with
one three-edge star triangle at each root vertex, decompose the edges of the
total graph of **any** cubic simple graph into 5|V(G)|/2 triangles. Thus the
packing half does not use triangle-freeness. The notable equality ingredient
is the matching-size triangle cover under the triangle-free hypothesis.
Avoid advertising the packing count alone as a new difficult result.

## Proposed manuscript wording

Use a brief attribution where the constructions occur:

> We use the Euler-tour coloring method from the maximum-cut literature
> [Guruswami 1999; see also Hanaka–Kobayashi–Sone, Section 6], keeping the
> exceptional odd Eulerian component explicit.

> For the finite bases we use the modular construction attributed to Sebő
> in [Munaro 2016, Theorem 3.3.2]. The recursive extension is chosen to match
> the formal development.

Retain conservative headline language:

> The triangle-free-root case appears in [Munaro 2016, Corollary 3.3.3].
> We have not located prior statements covering all finite simple roots for
> either graph operator.

These are proposed author prose, not quotations from the sources.

## Search coverage and residual uncertainty

Queries combined Tuza / triangle packing / triangle transversal with line
graphs, total graphs, claw-free, quasi-line, locally co-bipartite, Euler
coloring, cubic, triangle-free, and the 5n/2 equality. Exact-title and citation
chain searches included the sources above, maximum-cut papers, and cycle
multiplicity papers. Searches covered material returned as available through
2026-10-08. No new all-line, all-total, or all-claw-free Tuza theorem was found.

Limitations: web indexing is incomplete; inaccessible papers, unpublished
work, alternate terminology, and an unnoticed short corollary remain possible.
The original Guruswami full text and Tuza 1990 full text were not read. No
expert was contacted. A formal kernel check cannot close these literature
gaps. Before a strong novelty or significance claim, obtain the original
maximum-cut paper and relevant specialist feedback. This review supports
preparing a cautiously worded submission for the author's review, not a claim
that originality or journal acceptance has been certified.
